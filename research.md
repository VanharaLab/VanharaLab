Our pulmonary research focuses on the mechanisms regulating lung epithelial plasticity, regeneration, and fibrosis. In collaboration with the Clinic of Respiratory Diseases and Tuberculosis, University Hospital Brno, we develop patient-specific models of interstitial lung diseases using induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) reprogrammed from peripheral blood mononuclear cells of patients with pulmonary fibrosis. These iPSCs are differentiated into early lung epithelial progenitor (ELEP) models to study disease-associated cellular responses, epithelial dysfunction, and impaired regenerative capacity. By combining patient-derived models with advanced imaging, molecular, histological, and bioanalytical approaches, we aim to identify mechanisms driving lung fibrosis and improve our understanding of chronic pulmonary diseases.


Our pancreatic research focuses on the biology of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) and the mechanisms regulating tumor progression and treatment response. In collaboration with the Surgery Clinic and the Department of Pathology, University Hospital Brno, we develop patient-derived models based on clinically characterized PDAC samples. Using a unique PDAC stromal biobank and associated clinical data, we investigate how tumor–stroma interactions, extracellular matrix (ECM) organization, and biomechanical properties of the tumor microenvironment influence disease progression and therapy response. We combine molecular, histological, and advanced bioanalytical approaches with 3D models, including spheroids and organoids, to characterize stromal remodeling and its impact on PDAC biology.

We are supported by the Czech Health Research Council, the Czech Science Foundation and the IOCB Foundation.
